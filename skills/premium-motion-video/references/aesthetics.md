# Aesthetics rulebook

Sources: Leo (@leomeethewoo, "Make your product videos look expensive (Apple Framework)", Sep 2026 —
50+ product videos for Trust Wallet, fomo, PrizePicks), analysis of his 7 showcase clips, and our own
client feedback on the "Nhà" and "Converse — The Pink Edit" films.

## 1. Brand = intention
- Premium brands don't sell the product in the video; they make people who already know the brand
  feel they chose right. Every choice needs a purpose: font, background, music, colour.
- **Premium is mostly what you leave out.** Most amateur videos fail by adding: extra effects, extra
  SFX, hype music. Set rules per video type and keep them across videos:
  - 3 colours (bg, fg, accent) — accent may be a 2-stop gradient
  - 1 background family (all-black, all-light, or strict black↔light alternation per shot)
  - 1 display face (tight tracking −0.02 to −0.035em) + 1 UI face
  - 1 music family per project type

## 2. Design before animation
- Storyboard key frames first (Leo's team: 5 designers storyboard every animation frame-by-frame in
  Figma before anyone animates). Our equivalent: the beat map + 3–4 stills gate.
- One shot = one idea. Key object centred. Every scene has air. Background supports, never competes.
- Type scale for 1080p: hero words 150–190 px, stats 280–360 px, captions 70–80 px, labels 40–56 px.
  If a thumbnail at 300 px wide can't be read, the text is too small.

## 3. Animation
1. **Smoothness** — ease every keyframe; overlap motions (stagger 0.08–0.25 beat). Linear = cheap.
2. **Dynamic transitions** — the next scene grows out of the previous one (shared element, push,
   rise, zoom-through a tile, iris). Direct cuts only when they serve the message.
3. **Adaptive rhythm** — speed up, slow down, stay; not a metronome. Typical 20–30 s promo:
   calm open (1 idea / 3–4 beats) → dense middle (1 idea / 2 beats) → air on the logo (5–6 beats).

Patterns seen in Leo's showcase (Apple / Trust Wallet / Infinity AI / cashback app):
- word-by-word kinetic headline ("Take / your / design / anywhere."), one accent word in gradient
- glow colour fields behind big stats ("UP TO **24-hour** BATTERY LIFE"), counters that count
- two-beat line swaps on black ("It's a" → "work of heart."), then product + wordmark + logo
- product hero centred on white with a slow push; UI cards floating, toggles flipping on the beat
- rolling odometer numbers (866 → 1000), deposit buttons, 3D coin/object bursts for fintech energy
- Design Layout vs Result: the storyboard frame and the final frame are almost identical — motion
  adds timing, not new design.

## 4. Music — BPM → feeling
| BPM | Feel | Use for |
|---|---|---|
| 60–80 | regal, cinematic, heritage | luxury, emotional brand films |
| 90–110 | smooth, cool, effortless | lifestyle, consumer apps |
| 115–123 | elite, kinetic, sophisticated | tech launches, keynote promos (default) |
| 124+ | drive, hype | sports, gaming — can destroy a premium feel |
Measure, don't trust the filename: the "120 BPM" plan for Converse was really a 115 BPM song.

## 5. Sound design
- Only SFX that feel important. After the mix, listen and remove anything too loud, out of place,
  or not helping the viewer understand the product.
- Whooshes: peak on the cut. UI clicks: onset on the click frame. Hits: on the logo landing.
- Levels: SFX peaks 12–20 dB under the music; loudness −14 LUFS integrated, −1.5 dBTP.
- Client feedback we got: "SFX too loud, too many 'ting ting'" → removed bell clusters, kept 1 SFX
  per transition at most.
